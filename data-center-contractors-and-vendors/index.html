The short answer

NLRC connects data center developers with qualified data center contractors, engineers, consultants and service providers. Developers submit project requirements, providers submit qualifications, and NLRC makes an introduction when service, geography, stage and experience align. NLRC is not a contractor or broker.

Introduction

NLRC is an engineering-led land origination company advancing powered land for data centers and AI infrastructure. A successful data center requires far more than land and power. Developers need experienced companies that can support site selection, environmental review, utility coordination, engineering, construction, commissioning, operations and long-term facility support. Finding those companies, market by market and stage by stage, is where projects lose months.

NLRC maintains a list of qualified service companies and makes introductions between developers and those companies, as a present service. Introductions are based on relevant experience, project stage, geography and facility scale. We support projects throughout the Midwest, Plains, Southwest and Mid-Atlantic, as well as strong projects nationwide.

NLRC is not a contractor or broker. We do not guarantee work, pricing, project awards or commercial outcomes. Parties remain responsible for their own evaluation, contracting and procurement decisions.

For Developers, Owners and Operators

Bring us the need. We bring back companies that fit it.

  • Matched on what matters: service category, project stage, geography and service radius, and facility scale in megawatts.
  • Verified experience, kept separate from general capability: a company that has built substations, commissioned live halls or serviced generators under load is matched on that record, not on a brochure.
  • Local knowledge: regional companies that know the permitting offices, the utilities, the soil and the workforce in the markets where projects are landing.
  • Every stage covered: site selection and due diligence, design and entitlement, power and construction, commissioning, operations, expansion and end of life.
  • The right route in: some scopes are hired direct, others flow through a general contractor, an engineering firm or a utility. We introduce through the route that fits how the work is actually bought.

For Contractors, Engineers and Consultants

Data center subcontractor opportunities: register once, get matched as needs arise

If your company serves data center projects, join the data center contractor network and be matched as needs arise.

  • Tell us your trade: your primary service, your secondary services, and whether the work is one-time project work, recurring service or both.
  • Tell us your ground: headquarters, branches and the radius you will actually travel. Regional strength is an advantage in this network, not a limitation.
  • Tell us your scale: the facility sizes you have served, and whether your experience is new construction, work in live facilities or both. AI and high-performance computing experience, including rack density and liquid cooling, is recorded on its own merits.
  • Emergency capability counts: companies that can answer a call at 2 AM are matched differently from companies that schedule planned work, and both are needed.

Registration places no obligation on either side. Where a fee applies to a specific introduction, it is disclosed in writing before any introduction is made. NLRC answers inquiries within 10 business days.

  4. Qualifications are checkedWhich may include licensing, insurance, safety information, references and demonstrated capability.

Why qualification comes first

Experience matters in mission-critical environments. Companies are evaluated on demonstrated capability rather than marketing claims. Qualification factors may include licensing, certifications, insurance, safety record, workforce capacity, references and data center experience. That evidence is checked before any introduction is made, so a short list from NLRC is a list worth calling.

Frequently asked questions

What does it cost to be listed as a service provider?

Registering your company, or submitting a project request, places no obligation on either side. Where a fee applies to a specific connection, it is disclosed in writing before any introduction is made.

What regions are covered?

Midwest, Plains, Southwest and Mid-Atlantic, and strong projects nationwide. Regional strength matters in matching, because diligence and construction are local work.

What size projects are supported?

From edge facilities to hyperscale campuses. Scale is part of every match, so a company is introduced to work sized to its record.

We are early. Our project is still at the land stage. Is it too soon?

No. The earliest needs, site studies, environmental due diligence, power studies and entitlement support, are categories in the network, and early introductions protect the schedule later.

Is NLRC a contractor or broker?

No. NLRC is an engineering-led land origination company that makes introductions between developers and service companies. It does not perform construction work.

Are introductions guaranteed?

No. Registration does not guarantee an introduction, and an introduction does not guarantee work or pricing.

Who sees the information I submit?

Your company profile or project request is shared only with parties matched to it, and only after you have confirmed on the form that you agree to be introduced. Contact details are not published on the site.

How long does review take?

NLRC answers inquiries within 10 business days.

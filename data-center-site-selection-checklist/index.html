Data center site selection checklist

The criteria a developer should confirm before committing to a site, ordered by what usually decides whether a project is buildable: power first, then gas, fiber, land, entitlement, environmental and title.

Request Powered Land Opportunities
The short answer

A data center site is ready to advance when it has a credible power pathway (transmission, substation and utility timeline), nearby natural gas and fiber, buildable land with the right zoning, clean environmental and title results, and a landowner aligned on structure. Confirm them in that order, because power failures cannot be fixed later.

Power and interconnection

Power is the first gate. A site that fails here does not recover.

  • Nearest high-voltage transmission line: voltage, distance and owner
  • Nearest substation: distance, ownership and available capacity headroom
  • Interconnection request status: queue position and the stage of any feasibility, system impact or facilities studies
  • Utility’s stated timeline and required network upgrades, and who pays for them
  • Fit between your load ramp schedule and what the utility can actually deliver
  • Behind-the-meter or on-site generation options as a bridge or a primary source

Natural gas

Gas matters for bridge power, backup generation and behind-the-meter designs.

  • Proximity, diameter and operator of the nearest interstate or major pipeline
  • Firm versus interruptible capacity available at the site
  • Air permitting path for on-site generation

Fiber and connectivity

Latency, route diversity and carrier choice decide whether a powered site is also a usable one.

  • Distance to long-haul backbone fiber
  • Number of carriers and physically diverse routes available
  • Cost and schedule to extend fiber to the property line

Land and physical characteristics

These drive civil cost and schedule.

  • Contiguous acreage sufficient for the first phase and expansion
  • Topography and grade, drainage and floodplain status
  • Soils and geotechnical conditions, and seismic exposure
  • Road access, and rail or heavy-haul access where equipment requires it

Zoning and entitlement

A site is not buildable until its use is allowed.

  • Current zoning classification and any industrial overlay
  • Rezoning or special-use process and the local government’s posture
  • Local requirements on noise, setbacks, height and water use

Environmental review

Environmental surprises are among the most common causes of late-stage delay.

  • Phase I environmental site assessment completed, with any recognized environmental conditions identified
  • Wetlands review and delineation where indicated
  • Habitat, protected species and stormwater considerations
  • Opportunities for ecological enhancement that support permitting and community alignment

Title, survey and ownership

Clean title and a clear owner relationship protect the whole project.

  • ALTA land title survey completed
  • Title commitment reviewed, including easements, encumbrances and mineral rights
  • Landowner alignment on the deal: option, ground lease, joint venture or sale
  • Access and utility easements secured or obtainable

Water and cooling

Cooling strategy determines water demand.

  • Source and availability of cooling water, or a closed-loop or dry cooling design
  • Wastewater handling and discharge permitting

Red flags that should stop a site early

  • No credible interconnection path that fits the project schedule
  • Wetlands or floodplain covering the buildable area
  • Unresolved title, easement or mineral rights issues
  • Zoning that cannot be changed, or a local government unwilling to engage
  • A landowner who is not aligned on structure or timing

Site selection questions answered

What is the most important factor in data center site selection?

Power. A data center needs a realistic, timely path to electricity at the scale of the project, so the first questions are how close the site is to high-voltage transmission and a substation, what capacity is available, and how long the utility will take to deliver it. Land, zoning and fiber matter, but they rarely rescue a site without a power pathway.

What is a Phase I environmental site assessment?

A Phase I environmental site assessment is a review of a property’s history, records and visible conditions to identify recognized environmental conditions, meaning signs of past or present contamination. Lenders, developers and utilities commonly require one before a site advances.

What is an ALTA survey?

An ALTA survey is a detailed land title survey prepared to ALTA/NSPS standards. It shows boundaries, easements, encroachments and improvements, and is used together with a title commitment to confirm what a buyer or lessee is actually getting.

What is utility interconnection feasibility?

Interconnection feasibility is an early engineering assessment of whether and how a proposed load or generator can connect to the grid at a specific location, including likely upgrades and rough timing. It is typically the first formal step with the utility, before more detailed system impact and facilities studies.

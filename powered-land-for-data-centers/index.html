The short answer

Powered land is land with a realistic path to electrical power at data center scale: near high-voltage transmission and a substation, with natural gas and long-haul fiber close by. Native Land & Resource Conservancy (NLRC) is an engineering-led land origination company that identifies, diligences and advances powered land for data center developers, and connects them directly with Brian Beauchamp, Co-Founder and AI Landman / Project Manager.

Why power, not land, is the data center bottleneck

Developers can raise capital and order equipment faster than utilities can deliver new capacity. Interconnection queues are constrained, transmission expansion lags demand, and many parcels that look good on a map turn out to have no workable path to power. The scarce asset is land that is already positioned for power, which is why NLRC starts with infrastructure first and acreage second.

NLRC partners with landowners to originate these sites early, then does the engineering and diligence work that normally eats months after a developer picks a parcel. The result is a pipeline of sites a developer can evaluate quickly, with landowner relationships already in place.

Frequently asked questions about powered land

What is powered land for data centers?

Powered land is land with a realistic path to electrical power at data center scale. In practice that means a parcel near high-voltage transmission and a substation, usually with natural gas and long-haul fiber close by, and with the zoning, environmental and landowner position needed to build. NLRC also uses the term power-enabled land for the same thing.

How does NLRC find and qualify powered land?

NLRC scores every candidate parcel with a proprietary 120-point framework covering transmission capacity, substation topology, natural gas access, fiber density, zoning and ecological characteristics. Sites that pass move through staged diligence (Tier I, Tier II, Tier III), which adds utility engagement, interconnection feasibility, Phase I environmental site assessments, ALTA surveys, geotechnical testing and wetlands review.

How much power can NLRC sites support?

NLRC targets sites capable of supporting 100 MW to gigawatt-scale deployments. For reference, the anonymized Central Plains parcel shown on this page is assessed as capable of a 150 to 400 MW initial deployment. Actual capacity for any site depends on utility engagement and interconnection studies.

What deal structures are available to data center developers?

NLRC works with developers through a ground lease, joint venture, equity participation or acquisition. NLRC partners with landowners rather than flipping land, so the structure is set per site, together with the landowner relationship already in place.

Which regions does NLRC cover?

NLRC supports projects throughout the Midwest, Plains, Southwest and Mid-Atlantic, as well as strong projects nationwide. Its pipeline example on this site is a Central Plains agricultural parcel. See the regional overview for how each region fits data center site selection.

Can I see specific available sites?

Site details are kept anonymized until a developer engages, to protect landowners and active diligence. Send your requirements through the form on this page and Brian Beauchamp will match them against the pipeline and walk you through the sites that fit.

Is NLRC a broker or a utility?

No. NLRC is an engineering-led land origination company. It originates and advances sites in partnership with landowners and introduces them to data center developers. It does not sell power, and it does not perform construction work.

I need contractors, engineers or consultants for a data center project. Can NLRC help?

Yes. NLRC maintains a list of qualified contractors, engineers, consultants and specialty service companies and makes introductions based on project needs, geography, stage and scale. See the contractor and vendor network.
